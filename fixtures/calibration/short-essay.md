# Why the city should keep the night bus

The night bus is not a luxury. It is how the hospital night shift, the bakery, and the airport cleaners get home.
Last year the route carried 1,800 riders on a typical weeknight and 2,400 on Fridays.

Cutting it would save about $400,000 a year. That figure assumes the drivers can be reassigned rather than paid off, which the union contract makes unlikely.
The last time a night route was cut, in 2019, evening rides on the connecting tram fell 12% within a quarter.

The proposal treats the bus as an empty vehicle after midnight. The counts say otherwise between 12:30 and 2:00, which is the shift change at the hospital.
A smaller saving, about $90,000, is available by ending the route at 1:30 instead of 3:00. That keeps the shift change and drops the nearly empty last hour.

I would keep the route and publish the hourly counts. The assumption to watch is the hospital shift time: if that moves to 11pm, the case for the later buses gets weaker.
